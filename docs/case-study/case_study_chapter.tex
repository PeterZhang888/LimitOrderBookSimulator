\section{Market-wide liquidity-shock case study}
\label{sec:liquidity-shock-case}

\subsection{Objective and hypotheses}

The purpose of the case study is to test whether a liquidity shock applied to
a small set of books affects otherwise unshocked books through a common
inventory-capacity constraint.  It is distinct from the scaling study.  The
scaling study asks whether one large coupled market can be executed efficiently;
the case study asks whether the implemented coupling generates an economically
interpretable cross-asset response.

Four predeclared hypotheses guide the analysis.  First, aggressive sell orders
should deteriorate liquidity in the targeted books.  Second, if a globally
constrained shared market maker is active, non-target books should exhibit a
larger deterioration than under an uncoupled market maker.  Third, this
difference should be stronger under the tighter global capacity.  Fourth,
clusters with wider spreads and lower depth should exhibit a larger response
than more liquid clusters.  The first hypothesis concerns successful shock
injection; the remaining hypotheses concern cross-book transmission.

\subsection{Experimental design}

The experiment uses the 30 January 2020 development-validation configuration for the balanced
panel of 1,480 NASDAQ symbols.  Each symbol is represented by one limit order
book and retains its symbol-specific background-order-flow inputs.  Behavioural
policies are frozen at the level of ten liquidity clusters.  The 6.5-hour
regular session contains 23,400 one-second decision windows.  Fifteen symbols
are selected with a fixed mask that covers every cluster.  At
$t^\star=11{,}700$ seconds, an aggressive sell order equal to one contemporaneous
top-of-book depth is submitted to each selected book.

The treatments are: (i) a globally constrained shared market maker with
capacity 25 per asset; (ii) the same mechanism with capacity 100; (iii) an
uncoupled specification that retains the liquidity-supply logic but removes
the common cross-asset capacity constraint; and (iv) a control without the
shared market maker.  Every treatment is run with shock on and shock off for
five entity-keyed seeds.  Shock and control paths use common random numbers,
giving 40 full-session paths in total.  Each path uses 32 MPI ranks.  The
target mask is fixed across treatments, but the number of submitted shares is
state dependent because it is defined as a multiple of contemporaneous depth.
Consequently, differences in the direct response across treatments are
descriptive and are not interpreted as responses to an identical share order.

For outcome $Y$, let $Y^1_{mcs}(t)$ and $Y^0_{mcs}(t)$ denote the mean of
non-target books in mode $m$, cluster $c$ and seed $s$ in shocked and control
paths.  Spread deterioration is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^S_{mcs}(t)=S^1_{mcs}(t)-S^0_{mcs}(t),
\end{equation}
whereas depth deterioration is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^D_{mcs}(t)=D^0_{mcs}(t)-D^1_{mcs}(t).
\end{equation}
Positive values therefore indicate worse liquidity.  The cross-asset estimand
is the paired cross-mode contrast, where $g$ and $u$ denote the global
and uncoupled modes,
\begin{equation}
  \tau^Y_{cs}(t)=\Delta^Y_{g,cs}(t)-\Delta^Y_{u,cs}(t),
\end{equation}
summarised over the 1,800 seconds following the shock.  This construction
removes seed-specific background variation and isolates effects attributable
to the global coupling.

\subsection{Mechanism audit}

The state of the shared market maker explains the cluster-level null.  As shown
in Table~\ref{tab:r36-capacity-audit}, the global quote scale becomes
permanently zero long before $t^\star$.  At capacity 25 this occurs after 2.41
minutes on average; at capacity 100 it occurs after 23.62 minutes.  The shock is
applied after 195 minutes.  The shared maker therefore supplies no liquidity
and absorbs no shock volume when the intervention occurs.

The implementation multiplies both risk-increasing and risk-reducing quotes by
the global scale.  Once that scale reaches zero, the dealer cannot trade to
reduce inventory; its inventory and utilisation cease to recover, making the
zero-quote state absorbing.  The primary difference-in-differences estimate is
therefore non-identifying for shared-liquidity contagion.  It shows that the
implemented channel was inactive, not that an active common dealer prevented
or failed to transmit the shock.  Hypotheses 2--4 cannot be evaluated from this
matrix.

\subsection{Computational evidence}

The rank-equivalence preflight processed 3,100,370
orders and produced the same state hash on one and 32 ranks.  Runtime fell from
8.412 to 0.638
seconds, a speedup of 13.19 and efficiency of
41.2\%.  This establishes that whole-book
decomposition changes execution time without changing the simulated state.
The 40 production paths processed 6,030,761,053
orders in total.

The science matrix is not used as the principal scaling benchmark.  Fourteen
paths experienced intermittent communication stalls of 235--471 seconds while
their compute time remained comparable with other paths.  Treatment wall times
would therefore confound the financial design with system noise.  Strong- and
weak-scaling conclusions are taken from the dedicated benchmark campaign,
whereas this section uses MPI to make the large coupled market practical and
to preserve causal equivalence across ranks.

\subsection{Limitations and case-study conclusion}

Three limitations determine the interpretation.  First, the 2020 session is a
development validation date rather than an untouched final holdout.  Second,
five seeds and one to three targets per cluster are insufficient for a precise
cluster-specific direct-effect ranking.  Third, the shared dealer is inactive
at the shock, so the central contagion mechanism is absent.  The cluster audit
is nevertheless informative: it demonstrates that the null is universal
across heterogeneous books and identifies the model state responsible for it.

The experiment therefore establishes successful shock injection, deterministic
MPI equivalence and broad cluster coverage, but it does not establish
cross-asset liquidity contagion.  A corrected dealer should continue to submit
risk-reducing inventory-unwind quotes at the global boundary while suppressing
only risk-increasing quotes.  A new pilot should require a positive quote scale
immediately before the intervention and positive shared-maker absorption before
the production matrix is repeated.
